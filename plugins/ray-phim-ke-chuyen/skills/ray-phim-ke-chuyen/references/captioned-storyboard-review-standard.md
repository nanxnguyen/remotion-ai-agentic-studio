## Review contact-sheet contract
1. **Default format:** 12 tall (roughly 9:16) storyboard cards in a carefully spaced 4×3 grid, on a neutral dark review background. Choose 16 in a 4×4 for complex stories or when a specified 16-shot review is requested. Keep a consistent title strip/top tag, image height, bottom synopsis lane, and timing metadata below each card (not on the imagery). Include short sequence numbering for tracking. Do not add arbitrary extra frames just to fill a grid.
2. **Hero headline:** bold cinematic condensed typography, 2–7 words in uppercase Vietnamese with perfect accents. One threat, clue, dramatic action or hook per card; 1–2 words in carefully chosen accent colors; often top of frame but never covering the only narrative evidence. Dramatic headline must be accurate to that shot and not contain invented twists.
3. **Story caption:** 6–14 words (1–2 readable lines) near the bottom, explaining action or revelation; meaningful denser storytelling than previous 2–4 word captions but not an unreadable paragraph. Maintain white-space and face/prop visibility. Prefer actual caption aligned to corresponding future voice/subtitle event.
4. **Visual proof:** an image that visibly depicts the before→action→result for each narrated event. Keep character age/wardrobe/props consistent. Vary establishing, medium, facial close-up, object detail, POV, over-shoulder, antagonist hint, and payoff shot. No repetitive generic portrait filler.
5. **Adaptive art direction:** derive a mini design system from the screenplay before drawing. Specify emotional temperature, base tones, light sources, material palette, and 1–2 narrative accent colors. Do not make red, yellow, blue, black, purple, or green mandatory across unrelated stories. Example directions (not requirements): storm/fog blue-grey+ivory; occult earth/moss+aged brass; gothic ritual plum+ash; industrial neon cyan+warning orange; folklore sepia+bloodless crimson. Choose font highlight hues after scene lighting and contrast tests, not before. Preserve legibility; no neon overload.
6. **Review-only UI:** mini-series label/section number, optional progress strip, timing markers (e.g. `02.0s`) placed *outside* each card in a consistent position. Those are for the review board, not footage. Do not use a full-screen view of the contact sheet as a movie scene.
7. **QA before showing:** mobile-size test on full 4-column board plus enlarged panels, proper diacritics, headline/caption fit, at least one distinct relevant on-screen element per card, consistent faces, escalating story rhythm; all required scenes present; no narrative-to-image mismatch.

## Final MP4 remains separate
- Render from **clean original per-shot media**, never screenshots or crops of a captioned storyboard panel.
- **No storyboard timecode, numbering, review header, review cards, review caption lane, or progress bar embedded in the video.** Only editorially intended title graphics and Vbee-matched native subtitles may appear.
- No opaque black obstructions over doors, hands, phones, or other critical evidence; captions live in safe areas, using subtle shadow/small gradient only.
- Native video subtitles are timed to measured real speech, not pasted storyboard story-captions as still frames. Preserve exact film duration, SFX and voice-to-visual evidence QA.
