# Human-centered Vietnamese comedy writing

## Core rule
Make the audience laugh because the situation is painfully familiar, then reward them with a useful insight. The product is the release from tension, not the punchline by itself.

## Audience-first analysis
Write one sentence for each item before drafting:

- **Moment:** What exact event makes the viewer sigh, panic or waste time?
- **Inner thought:** What would they actually mutter in that moment?
- **Cost:** What happens next—rework, embarrassment, overtime, lost money or confusion?
- **Desire:** What simple outcome would feel like relief?
- **Trust:** What proof would make the viewer believe the product helps?

Turn abstract capabilities into lived consequences:

| Abstract | Human-centered |
| --- | --- |
| Detects schema changes | Báo trước khi một field đổi tên kéo cả giao diện đi bụi |
| Automates research | Đỡ mở 27 tab rồi quên mình đang tìm gì |
| Improves productivity | Bớt một tối ngồi dò lỗi cùng ly cà phê đã nguội |

## Comedy palette
Choose one primary device and use one callback at most.

- **Relatable exaggeration:** Magnify a real inconvenience without inventing product claims.
- **Deadpan contrast:** State an absurd consequence calmly.
- **Self-aware aside:** Let the narrator admit a familiar mistake or limitation.
- **Everyday analogy:** Explain technical behavior through food, traffic, weddings, group chat or office life.
- **Mini-character:** Give the bug, API or AI one simple motive; do not misrepresent how it works.
- **Callback:** Reuse one phrase or visual near the payoff so the ending feels earned.

## Natural Vietnamese dialogue
- Write for the ear: one idea per breath, usually 5–14 words.
- Use everyday connectors and particles only where natural: “thì”, “mà”, “nha”, “chứ”, “ủa”, “khoan”.
- Mix short and medium lines. Put a micro-pause before the punch word.
- Underplay the joke. Prefer “API đổi một field thôi… frontend thì mở tiệc báo đỏ” over a loud generic claim.
- Let important facts stay plain. Do not turn every sentence into a joke.

## Humor safety
- Target the broken process, confusing interface, bug or narrator's own predicament.
- Keep cheekiness playful, not vulgar, humiliating, discriminatory or hostile.
- Never use humor to hide uncertainty, prerequisites, security boundaries or limitations.
- Avoid meme chains, trend slang with no audience fit, fake quotes and fabricated usage stories.

## Example transformation

**Flat:** “DevMirror records API requests, detects breaking changes and generates fixtures.”

**Human-centered:** “Backend đổi `total` từ số thành chữ rồi đi ngủ rất ngon. Frontend sáng mai mở máy thì… cây thông Noel báo đỏ. DevMirror ghi lại request, chỉ đúng chỗ response vừa đổi và đóng luôn case đó thành fixture để bạn test lại—trước khi bug kịp lên staging.”

The second version still communicates the same verified capabilities, but it begins with a familiar conflict, explains the mechanism through consequences, and lands the benefit as relief.
