## 4. Verified Primary Bibliography (Session-Verified Dossier)

1. **Spring Impact & Tanis Event (`15–30 phút`, `3.000 km`)**
   - `During et al. (2022), Nature` — Osteohistology and stable isotope records in Tanis paddlefish and sturgeon bones confirm the Chicxulub impact occurred during **Northern Hemisphere spring**.
   - `DePalma et al. (2019), PNAS` — Seismic waves from Chicxulub reached the Western Interior Seaway riverbed (`~3,000 km` / `3.000 km` away) within `~15–30 minutes` (`15–30 phút`), triggering standing **seiche waves** that buried fish alive while impact glass spherules (tektites) rained down and lodged inside fish gills.

2. **Impactor Size, Crater, Day-1 Fill & Ocean Resurge (`10–12 km`, `180–200 km`, `130 m`, `>30 phút`)**
   - `Gulick et al. (2019), PNAS` (*"The first day of the Cenozoic"*) — IODP–ICDP Expedition 364 peak-ring drill core shows `~130 m` (`130 m`) of impact melt rock and tsunami resurge breccia deposited inside the `~180–200 km` (`180–200 km`) Chicxulub crater (carved by a `~10–12 km` / ~6-mile impactor) within **a single day**. Ocean tsunamis radiated outward and surged back into the crater starting within `~30+ minutes` to hours (`>30 phút`). Absence of sulfur-rich target rocks in the core confirms massive sulfate aerosol vaporization into the stratosphere.

3. **Near-Field Blast & Thermal Radiation Reach (`1.500 km`)**
   - Chicxulub impact timelines (`Kring / Gulick`) — Direct blast wave and near-field thermal radiation pulse extended over `900 miles` (`~1,450–1,500 km`; `1.500 km`).

4. **Global Fine Silicate Dust Thermal Lid ("Global Oven", `1–3 giờ` / `1–2+ hours`)**
   - `Brandon C. Johnson et al. (Purdue University), Journal of Geophysical Research: Biogeosciences` (published July 28, 2026 — *"Heat and Wildfires during the K-Pg Mass Extinction Enhanced by Fine Dust"*) — Impact vaporized `>1,000 km³` of rock into molten spherules and ultra-fine silicate dust. As spherules re-entered the atmosphere, fine dust acted as a thermal blanket/lid trapping infrared heat downward, turning Earth's surface into a planetary oven within the first `1–2+ hours` (`1–3 giờ` surface oven window; `~17x` lethal human radiation threshold) and igniting global wildfires; confirms survival strongly favored **underground burrows** and **aquatic shelters**.

5. **Far-Field Hour-1 Sky Color Shift (Asia, Europe, New Zealand)**
   - `Michael Benton` (paleontologist) & `Monica Grady` (planetary scientist), reported in *The Guardian* / *Hayadan* — Far across Asia, Europe, and New Zealand, Hour 1 brought little immediate physical destruction aside from an eerie, unnatural change in sky color as dust and soot began spreading around the globe.

6. **Multi-Year Impact Winter Cooling (`ít nhất 26°C`) & Extinction (`75%`)**
   - `Brugger et al. (2017), Geophysical Research Letters` (Potsdam Institute) — Sulfate aerosols and soot blocked sunlight for years, plunging global annual mean surface air temperature **by at least 26°C** (`ít nhất 26°C`; tropics ~27°C -> ~5°C; global annual mean below freezing for roughly 3 years), driving `~75%` (`75%`) species extinction.

7. **Burrow Thermal Insulation, Mammal Ecology, Detrital Food Web & Avian Survival**
   - `Robertson et al. (2004)` — Underground burrows functioned as a thermal shelter shielding survivors from the hours-long infrared radiation pulse from re-entering ejecta, fine dust, and initial surface heat shock.
   - `Chester, Bloch, Boyer, & Clemens (2015), PNAS` — Tarsal ankle bones of *Purgatorius* exhibit high joint mobility specialized for **arboreal (tree-dwelling)** locomotion; therefore *Purgatorius* is NOT depicted as a ground burrower. Episode 3 instead depicts unnamed **small Late Cretaceous burrowing mammals** (e.g., fossorial multituberculates / burrowing insectivores).
   - `Vajda & McLoughlin (2004)` — The K–Pg boundary fungal spike fueled by massive plant die-off established a **detrital food web** (fungi, earthworms, burrowing insects/grubs independent of sunlight). Small burrowing mammals survived on stored seeds/insects, while carnivorous pocket *T. rex* Tí Hon eats earthworms and grubs in damp burrow soil (never seeds).
   - **Avian dinosaurs (*Aves*):** Living theropod dinosaur clade that survived the K–Pg mass extinction.
