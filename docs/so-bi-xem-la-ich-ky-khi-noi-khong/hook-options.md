## H1 engineering detail

1. **0–1 s literal visual:** no logo or fade. The viewer sees `21:47`, the request, and a thumb that has typed `Ok` but cannot press Send. The upper-third text is readable while muted; nothing sits in the bottom 210 px.
2. **1–3 s promise:** `Tôi nhận thêm việc dù đã kiệt sức` arrives by about 1.95 s at 4.1 syllables/s; the phone action supplies the rest while the full required line continues. At 1.70 s, the remembered sentence enters as an italic mono quote, visibly memory rather than another speaker.
3. **First payoff:** by 5.13 s the reply is visibly not sent; `s2` reveals the work tabs at 7.8 s and `s3` expands that capacity cost with task rows, dark office, and third coffee. This pays “why can’t they press Send?” before second 8 without an unsupported diagnosis.
4. **Anti-clickbait guardrail:** H1 does not promise that saying no repairs a relationship or workload. `s6` shows only one response, and `s7` resolves the opening by closing the laptop and holding silence.

Research candidates H3 and H4 are not selected: H3 begins with the internal fear without the observable 21:47 action; H4 has a strong lone-desk picture but postpones the request and personal stake. No candidate uses a statistic, diagnosis, or forced shock claim.
