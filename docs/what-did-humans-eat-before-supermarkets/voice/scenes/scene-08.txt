Much later, some human communities began cultivating plants and managing domesticated animals.

Agriculture did not begin everywhere at the same time. It developed through different processes in different regions.

Producing food could support more settled communities, but it also introduced new responsibilities.

Crops had to be tended. Animals needed care. Harvests had to be managed, and a poor season could threaten the food supply.

Over time, farming helped reshape settlements, landscapes, and the organization of work.

This was not a single moment when everyone stopped hunting and became a farmer.

Different ways of life continued to coexist.

Human food history is a story of gradual change, local adaptation, and many different solutions to the same basic need.
