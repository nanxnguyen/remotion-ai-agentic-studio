Nara begins with what the landscape can offer.

Wild plants may provide fruits, seeds, nuts, roots, and other edible resources. Animals can provide meat and fat. Rivers and coastlines can offer fish and shellfish where those resources are available.

But finding something edible requires knowledge.

Which plants are safe? Which foods are seasonal? Where are animals likely to move? Which resources need careful preparation?

There was no single menu shared by all prehistoric people.

A community living near a river faced different choices from one living in a dry grassland or a colder region.

Food depended on local conditions, accumulated knowledge, and the tools people could use.

Nara gathers what can be safely identified, but the basket is still far from full.
