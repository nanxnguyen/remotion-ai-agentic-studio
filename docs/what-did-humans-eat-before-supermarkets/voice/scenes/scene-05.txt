Now travel much further back in time.

At Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, in what is now Israel, researchers found evidence suggesting that freshwater fish had been cooked around seven hundred and eighty thousand years ago.

The evidence includes ancient fish remains associated with hearth-related activity, alongside analyses indicating exposure to relatively low temperatures.

The researchers interpreted these findings as evidence of cooking by early human relatives.

This does not mean modern humans were necessarily responsible, nor does it show that all early human groups cooked fish.

It reveals something more precise: at this particular site, hundreds of thousands of years ago, early hominins were likely using heat to prepare aquatic food.
