# Hook engineering for 30–45 second vertical videos

Sources: YouTube Shorts deep dive (2025), TikTok Business video advertising guide, TikTok creative best practices. This is a **creative rubric**, not a guaranteed virality formula.

## Production contract

- 0–1 s: a first-frame visual with an observable subject, problem, consequence, or surprising result. No logo animation, title-only fade or generic greeting.
- 1–3 s: one precise curiosity gap or truthful value promise expressed in speech and readable on-screen text.
- 3–8 s: first concrete payoff/proof. Never withhold all value to inflate watch time.
- 8–20 s: progress by concrete examples, contrast, demonstration or story turn.
- Around 15–20 s when runtime permits: a meaningful re-hook rather than repeating the opening claim.
- End: resolve the original promise and deliver one retellable takeaway; optional CTA only after payoff.

## Candidate patterns (choose by profile)

| Pattern | When | Example skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| Result first, mechanism second | knowledge/product | Show surprising result → ask why → prove cause |
| Common mistake → specific fix | tutorial/product | Show actual mistake → observable damage → correction |
| Before/after with truthful evidence | product/knowledge | Same conditions → meaningful contrast → explanation |
| Counterintuitive but verifiable question | news/knowledge | Factual surprising contrast → source → answer |
| In-media-res action | story | Begin inside a concrete event → stakes → earned outcome |

## Mandatory five-hook selection

Research proposes at least five substantially distinct candidates; script-agent selects at least three for written comparison, not five synonyms. For each candidate write:
1. The literal first-frame subject/action and 9:16 safe-zone text.
2. Spoken line 0–3s, estimated syllables and whether the viewer understands it with sound off.
3. Specific viewer tension/promise, verifiable claim source where relevant.
4. The concrete first payoff before second 8 and where the closing payoff resolves it.
5. Anti-clickbait risk and an explicit reason for rejecting weaker variants.

Save this as `docs/<vid>/hook-options.md`; the selected candidate ID is referenced in `script.md`, `brief.md`, and the gate A report. A candidate does not pass merely because it sounds dramatic.

## Gate A hook acceptance

Reviewer checks first frame, the literal 0–1s spoken/text timing, payoff by ~8s, sound-off legibility, fidelity to the user's subject and accuracy of promises. Hook sub-score >= 9 is a reviewer-side requirement; script overall >= 9 still applies. Reviewer must name a specific competing candidate and explain why the winner is better.

Common blockers: generic opening, title animation occupying first second, unsupported statistic, misleading before/after, withheld answer, a hook unrelated to the final payoff, text outside phone safe-zone or too long to read.

## Experiment after publication

Record opening pattern and visual in `docs/<vid>/hook-experiment.md`. If analytics are accessible, track YouTube Shorts `How many chose to view` (viewed versus swiped away), audience retention around 0–3s, and average view duration. Compare against videos with similar topic/platform/audience rather than inventing universal target percentages. Do not assert a formula is trending solely from anecdotal view counts.
