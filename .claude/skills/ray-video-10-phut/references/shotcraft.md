# Short-form cinematic shotcraft — visual storytelling, not random effects

## A five-part decision for every shot

1. **Purpose**: reveal context, prove a claim, show emotion, create contrast or land the payoff.
2. **Framing**: subject scale (wide/medium/detail), safe caption region, image hierarchy and screen direction.
3. **Action**: exactly one salient visual event per beat; body movement or object logic must be physically legible.
4. **Transition**: cut/dissolve/match/mask as a meaningful relationship between shots, not a transition generator showcase.
5. **Sound**: narration is foreground; one meaningful SFX on a reveal/impact, bed music chosen for mood and not masking language.

## Four useful classic motion patterns

- **Match cut**: similar subject shape/motion bridges from question to answer; preserve continuity of position/scale.
- **Push-in**: attention increases as a claim becomes specific; limit amplitude so small captions stay legible.
- **Parallax reveal**: near/far layers expose depth; use as illustration rather than fake source evidence.
- **Graphic transformation**: one visual encodes the conceptual change (before/after, compare, map, timeline). More valuable than generic card stacking.

Do not overuse one technique. Build a motion motif consistent with the art direction card; use at most two main transition families in a 30–45s video unless demonstrably beneficial. Do not claim that these patterns guarantee retention.

## Hook package: four tangible proof surfaces

- Frame 0 cover: recognizable subject, concise text, strong contrast.
- Motion strip: visible visual change every few frames through opening; no stagnant clone tiles.
- Audio: first sentence/stress mapped to one visual reveal without unnatural speech acceleration.
- Sound-off read: within 2s viewer can infer subject/promise from images/captions.

## Tempo and editorial rhythm

The edit is not one continuous animation. Contrast moving elements with controlled pauses. Emotional beats need dwell time; technical demos need sufficient detail; avoid repeating identical scene arrangements. Re-hook about 15–20s when it serves the story. Validate real transition boundary frames rather than relying on a storyboard.

## Safe interpretation

For news/knowledge/product, clearly differentiate authentic footage, verified quantitative visualization and fictional illustration. Do not stage a metaphor as factual proof or misrepresent a person/product. Record sources and media usage rights.
